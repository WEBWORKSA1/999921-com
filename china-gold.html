The big shift: from jewellery to bars

In 2025 Chinese buyers spent more on gold than ever (¥796 bn, +53%), yet jewellery tonnage fell 25% while bars and coins jumped 28%. Record prices pushed households toward investment gold — 9999 bars, coins and 1-gram gold beans — where premiums are thin and resale is easy. Jewellery spending still rose 8% by value to ¥281.4 bn: people bought lighter pieces at higher prices.

That trend continued into 2026: China’s H1 bar & coin demand hit 314 t, the strongest first half on record, while Q2 jewellery fell to 50 t, the lowest Q2 since 2004.

The Shanghai Gold Exchange

Why numbers matter to Chinese gold buyers

  • 9 (九 jiǔ) sounds like 久 “long-lasting” — the emperor’s number. Legend says the Forbidden City has 9,999½ rooms (the real count is 9,371).
  • 8 (八 bā) sounds like 发 “prosper”. The phone number 88888888 sold for ¥2.33 m in 2003; Hong Kong plate “28” fetched HK$18.1 m in 2016.
  • 520 / 1314 — “I love you / for a lifetime” — drive gold sales on 20 May and Qixi.
  • 4 (四 sì) sounds like 死 — avoided in weights, prices and gifts.
