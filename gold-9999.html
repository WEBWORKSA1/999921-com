What is 9999 gold? “Four nines” explained

99.99% pure. The standard of the world’s largest physical gold exchange — and the first half of 999921.

Quick answer: 9999 gold is gold with at least 999.9 parts per 1,000 of pure gold — no more than 0.01% other metals. In China it’s stamped 足金9999 (older name 千足金); in bullion it’s “four nines fine.”

The difference between 9999 and 999 is just 0.09% gold — about — per 100 g at today’s price. It matters more for trust, liquidity and exchange eligibility than for raw value.

Where 9999 is traded

The Shanghai Gold Exchange (SGE), launched by the People’s Bank of China on 31 October 2002, is the world’s largest physical gold exchange. It accepts gold at 9999, 9995, 999 and 995 purity, and its flagship Au99.99 contract (10 g lots, quoted in yuan per gram) is built on 1 kg bars of at least 99.99% purity.

Popular 9999 bars & coins

  • 1 kg & 100 g bars — lowest premium per gram; favoured by serious investors.
  • 1 oz sovereign coins such as the Canadian Maple Leaf and Chinese Panda (the Panda is sold in grams since 2016).
  • Gold beans (金豆豆) — 1 g pellets hugely popular with young Chinese savers.
  • Zodiac & gift bars — higher premium, great for weddings and 红包.

Reading Chinese hallmarks

Under China’s national standard GB 11887, jewellery labelled 足金 must be at least 990‰. A 2014 revision (effective around 2016) retired the term 千足金, so today you’ll see 足金999 or 足金9999, often with the maker’s mark and a testing-lab stamp.

Should you pay more for 9999?

  • For investment: yes, if the premium difference is under ~0.5% — 9999 resells everywhere.
  • For jewellery: not necessarily. 99.99% gold is very soft; 22K/21K wears better daily.
  • For gifting: 9999 bars and beans carry the “forever” symbolism — 9 = 久.
